Mulighetsstudie · app.builtly.ai

What can the plot carry — and does it add up?

From property to decision basis: the plot is fetched from the cadastre, the zoning is read, the buildable area is drawn straight on the orthophoto — and the engine builds complete development alternatives with areas, homes, sun and shadow and project economics. Out comes a report for the board and the bank, and a teaser for everyone who should understand the project without jargon.

§ 01The basisYou give the address — the rest is fetched

Measured, never assumed.

01The plot

The cadastral parcel as a polygon.

Search by address or municipality, gnr and bnr — the plot arrives as its actual polygon, every parcel included, with the area measured from the geometry rather than read from a field. No property in the register? Upload your own polygon, and the report states the source.

02The zoning

The plan is the frame — with the denominator declared.

Land use, utilisation, building heights and building limits where they exist as map layers. "60 % BYA" means nothing until you know 60 % of what — so the study always states what the utilisation is measured against, and uses the same denominator through the whole report.

03Neighbours & terrain

Shadows cast by the houses that actually stand there.

Neighbouring buildings arrive with footprints and heights, and the terrain as an elevation model. If a source does not answer, the surface says "unknown" — the terrain slope is never a guess.

04Orthophoto

Fresh aerial imagery — and the historical archive.

A current orthophoto underlies the drawing and the illustrations, with historical aerial photos where they exist — so you also see what the plot has been.

§ 02The buildable areaAlmost no plot is buildable in its full extent

Draw the truth onto the plot.

  • 01
    A stream, a power line, a slope — drawn straight on the orthophoto
    Points snap to the plot boundary, shift-click follows the boundary with its own corners, and the area you have drawn is shown in m² while you work. The drawing is coordinate-exact: what you see is what the engine computes on.
  • 02
    A real building limit is not the same as a buffer
    Where the plan has an actual building-limit layer, it is used. Where it does not, you set a distance requirement — and the surface says which of the two applies, because they give different buildable areas on the same plot.
  • 03
    Study a part of the property
    One construction stage, one parcel, the area north of the road — draw the cut-out the same way. It is clipped against the cadastral parcel automatically, so a study plot can never be larger than the property you own.
§ 03The alternativesArchitecturally real — never random boxes

Seven typologies. Complete alternatives.

  • 01
    Slab, point block, row, courtyard cluster, perimeter block, tower, podium + tower
    Each alternative is built on one typology with realistic dimensions: slabs deep enough for through-lit apartments, point blocks free-standing, perimeter blocks making real courtyards. Areas, homes, outdoor space, parking, sun and economics are computed for every one.
  • 02
    The typology is never blended away
    An alternative called perimeter block IS a perimeter block all the way — the engine never fills leftover area with another typology to squeeze in more square metres. The concept must be readable in the plan.
  • 03
    The number of homes is a consequence, not a wish
    You state the apartment mix as a distribution with typical sizes — and the engine computes the number of homes from the area each alternative actually yields.
  • 04
    Master plan and construction stages
    Large plots are split into stages: you set the order and content per stage, the study shows the consequence for cash flow and execution, and the stages are drawn in the plan.
§ 04CommercialOffice, logistics, hotel and retail — own programmes

Not just housing.

01Programme profiles

Each use carries its own dimensions.

Logistics is planned with 12 m clear height and retail with 4.5 m; offices at 3.6 m and hotels at 3.2 m floor height — with each programme's building depths, storey spans and parking norm, each stated with its source. A warehouse never gets courtyards, playgrounds or an apartment mix.

02Logistics is measured

"Fits", "tight" or "does not fit" — with both numbers.

The plot is tested against four measured requirements: manoeuvring depth in front of the docks, loading-façade length, circulation around the building and staging area — the need is a norm with a source, the space is a measurement of your concept. What cannot be measured says "not measured", never a green check on an assumption.

03Yield economics

Commercial is valued on rent and yield — on BTA.

An office building is not sold apartment by apartment. You set rent per m² per year and a yield requirement per use, the mix splits the area across housing, office, hotel, industry and retail — and the report shows the income basis per use and a yield sensitivity for the land value.

04Honest throughout

"Programme: Office" — never "0 homes".

Sellable is called lettable when the programme is commercial, outdoor-space requirements are reported "not assessed" for pure commercial programmes, and the illustrations draw the programme: loading docks for logistics, office façades for offices — never balconies on a warehouse.

§ 05The recommendationA score nobody can inspect cannot be verified

It says why — with both numbers.

  • 01
    The score's composition is public
    Hit against the area target 34 %, sun and daylight 28 %, open outdoor space 18 %, area efficiency 14 %, parking pressure 6 %. Sun and shadow are computed for equinox and both solstices at several times of day, and the share of sunlit outdoor space is one of the numbers the alternatives are ranked on.
  • 02
    Delivering too little is punished harder than too much
    Half the volume is not half as good an alternative — it is another project. An alternative above the target can be scaled down and is punished at half weight. Without that asymmetry, a low, sprawling concept could win on sun and outdoor space with half the volume.
  • 03
    "Recommended because" — in plain text
    Below the comparison table stands the criterion that actually decided, with both numbers: "hits the target volume best (97 % against 62 % for Alternative B)". And when the recommendation does not win on volume, the volume loss stands right next to it — that is exactly the trade-off you are meant to weigh.
  • 04
    When it is close, the choice is architectural
    If two alternatives are near each other, the report says so in plain text: then the choice between them is a professional judgement, not a ranking. The score is decision support — it never crowns a winner on the architect's or the developer's behalf.
§ 06The economicsThe answer to "what can we bid?"

Residual land value, assumptions visible.

01Sales income

Price per square metre times sellable area, with market price references from around the plot as a starting point you can override.

02Construction cost

Per m² BTA, differentiated where the typology demands it — a tower does not cost the same as row houses.

03Client costs

Broker, fees, consultants, financing and contingency — proposed as line items you adjust one by one.

04Residual land value

What remains when the costs and the profit requirement are covered — what the plot can bear to cost for the project to add up. Every rate can be overridden, and the report prints the assumptions next to the result.

NB

The valuation is an early-phase calculation, not an appraisal. It shows how the economics react to the assumptions — use it to steer, and to see which numbers the project is most sensitive to.

§ 07The deliverablesSame numbers — two very different readers

A report for the bank. A teaser for the neighbours.

01The complete report

The document that goes to the board and the bank.

Full PDF with table of contents: property and zoning, buildable area, every alternative with plan illustration and key figures, sun and shadow, comparison table, valuation and assumptions. The prose is drafted by AI from the study's own numbers — the figures are always computed deterministically first, and the text is read as a draft from an adviser.

02The teaser

The same project, explained without jargon.

For politicians, neighbours and the local community: rounded numbers ("about 330 homes"), the concept drawn into the plot with neighbours and outdoor space — and not one number in the illustrations. The point is to be understood, not to document.

Honesty

Measured, never guessed — where a source is missing, it says "unknown". The source follows every number, and what you have overridden is stated. AI proposes, the human decides. And the study says clearly what it is not: not detailed design, not a TEK17 daylight calculation, not an appraisal, not a plan interpretation.
